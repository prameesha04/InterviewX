INTERVIEW EVALUATION GUIDELINES

=== SCORING RUBRIC (0–10) ===

Score 9–10 (Excellent):
- Answer directly addresses the question with no ambiguity
- Demonstrates deep understanding of the concept
- Includes concrete examples or code snippets where appropriate
- Shows awareness of trade-offs, edge cases, and alternatives
- Answer is well-structured (uses STAR for behavioral, explanation→example→implication for technical)
- Goes beyond the basics to demonstrate genuine expertise
- Uses precise technical vocabulary correctly

Score 7–8 (Good):
- Answer correctly addresses the main question
- Demonstrates solid understanding with minor gaps
- Includes a relevant example but may lack specificity
- Structure is clear but could be more organized
- Vocabulary is appropriate; occasional imprecision
- Shows awareness of some trade-offs

Score 5–6 (Adequate):
- Answer is partially correct; covers the surface but misses depth
- Understanding is present but with noticeable gaps
- Example is generic or missing
- Answer could be more focused or structured
- Some technical terms used incorrectly or imprecisely
- Does not address trade-offs or edge cases

Score 3–4 (Needs Improvement):
- Answer is mostly incorrect or incomplete
- Shows only surface-level understanding
- No concrete example or example is irrelevant
- Answer is disorganized or rambling
- Multiple technical misconceptions present
- Does not address what was actually asked

Score 1–2 (Poor):
- Answer barely addresses the question
- Significant factual errors or contradictions
- No structure or coherence
- Critical concepts misunderstood or confused
- Very limited vocabulary in the subject area

Score 0 (No Answer / Completely Incorrect):
- No attempt or completely off-topic
- Demonstrates no understanding of the subject

=== EVALUATION DIMENSIONS ===

1. TECHNICAL ACCURACY
- Are facts, definitions, and concepts correct?
- Is terminology used precisely?
- Are any statements misleading or false?

2. DEPTH OF UNDERSTANDING
- Does the candidate explain the "why" not just the "what"?
- Do they mention underlying mechanisms?
- Do they understand implications and consequences?

3. PRACTICAL APPLICATION
- Are real-world examples included?
- Does the candidate connect theory to practice?
- Do they mention tools, frameworks, or actual implementation details?

4. COMMUNICATION CLARITY
- Is the answer logically structured?
- Is it easy to follow?
- Does it stay on topic?
- Is the length appropriate (not too brief, not rambling)?

5. CRITICAL THINKING
- Does the candidate mention trade-offs?
- Do they acknowledge limitations?
- Do they compare alternatives?
- Do they think about edge cases?

=== FEEDBACK FORMULATION GUIDELINES ===

Strengths (what to identify):
- Correct use of technical terminology
- Clear and specific examples
- Structured answer format
- Mention of trade-offs
- Practical insights from experience or projects

Weaknesses (what to identify):
- Missing key concepts
- Factual inaccuracies (state the correct fact)
- Lack of examples
- Poor structure or focus
- Generic answers without specifics
- Missing trade-offs or depth

Model Answer (what to include):
- Complete, accurate answer to the question
- Well-organized structure
- Key technical points with brief explanations
- Concrete example or use case
- Mention of at least one trade-off or alternative

Recommendations (how to phrase):
- Be specific and actionable ("Study the difference between X and Y using [resource type]")
- Suggest practice approaches ("Practice implementing X from scratch to solidify understanding")
- Reference specific areas ("Review database indexing strategies, particularly B-tree internals and composite indexes")
- Be encouraging but honest ("This is a strong foundation — to elevate to 9/10, add a concrete project example")

=== BEHAVIORAL ANSWER EVALUATION ===

STAR Method Assessment:
- Situation: Is the context clear and relevant?
- Task: Is the candidate's specific responsibility clear?
- Action: Are specific, individual actions described (not "we did X")?
- Result: Is there a concrete, measurable outcome?

Red flags in behavioral answers:
- Uses "we" throughout without clarifying personal contribution
- Vague or generic answers without specifics
- No clear outcome or resolution
- Speaks negatively about colleagues or managers
- Chooses trivial or irrelevant situations

Green flags in behavioral answers:
- Specific names, dates, technologies, or metrics
- Clear personal ownership ("I decided to...", "I reached out to...")
- Reflects on what they would do differently (growth mindset)
- Quantified results ("reduced by 25%", "completed 2 weeks early")

=== HR ANSWER EVALUATION ===

Evaluate for:
- Authenticity and self-awareness
- Preparation and research (company knowledge questions)
- Career narrative coherence
- Professionalism and communication skills
- Cultural fit signals (collaboration, ownership, growth mindset)

Common HR answer weaknesses:
- Generic, templated answers that could apply to any company
- Salary expectations without market research basis
- "I work too hard" type fake weakness answers
- "I just want to learn" without specifics in motivation questions
- No questions asked when given the opportunity ("I have no questions")

=== SCORE ADJUSTMENT GUIDELINES ===

For entry-level / fresh graduates:
- Expect less hands-on experience; credit strong project examples
- Credit theoretical knowledge applied to hypothetical scenarios
- Reward clear learning trajectory and self-study evidence
- Be more lenient on "lack of professional experience" gaps

For mid-level candidates:
- Expect concrete professional examples
- Penalize generic answers more; experience should produce specifics
- Expect awareness of broader system impact

For senior-level candidates:
- Expect mentorship, architectural, and cross-team examples
- Expect discussion of organizational impact, not just individual impact
- Expect nuanced trade-off analysis
